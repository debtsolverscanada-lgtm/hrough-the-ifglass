## Fact check
- **Seeing requires absorbing light.** Rod and cone photoreceptors contain pigments (e.g. rhodopsin) that must absorb photons to trigger the visual signal; a perfectly transparent retina could not see. Source: Purves et al., *Neuroscience* (Sinauer), chapter on phototransduction. The "invisible man would be blind" point is a standard critique of H. G. Wells's *The Invisible Man* (1897).
- **Transparent objects are visible because light bends at their edges (refraction / refractive index mismatch).** An object vanishes when its refractive index matches its surroundings (classic demo: Pyrex glass in vegetable oil). Source: Hecht, *Optics*, 5th ed.
- **Proprioception and visual guidance of the hands.** We sense limb position without vision, but fine hand movements rely heavily on visual feedback. Source: Proske & Gandevia, "The proprioceptive senses," *Physiological Reviews* 92 (2012).
- **Red-eye effect.** Flash light reflects off the blood-rich choroid at the back of the eye and returns toward the source when the light is close to the camera/viewer axis. Source: American Academy of Ophthalmology, "Red eye in photos" (aao.org); Hecht, *Optics*.
- **Humans emit infrared; thermal cameras see body heat.** Skin emits mostly around 9–10 micrometres wavelength. Source: NASA Science, "Infrared Waves" (science.nasa.gov).
- **Glass frogs hide red blood cells in the liver while sleeping**, becoming two to three times more transparent. Source: Taboada et al., "Glassfrogs conceal blood in their liver to maintain transparency," *Science* 378 (2022).
- **Fiction, flagged in-script:** the lens, the storm, "his eyes stay visible," "anything he holds turns invisible," and "the power only covers visible light" are stated as "in this world" rules.
