## Fact check
- **The brain's body map / homunculus.** Wilder Penfield electrically stimulated the cortex of awake patients during epilepsy surgery (local anaesthetic; the brain itself feels no pain) and mapped the somatosensory and motor strips; hands, lips and tongue take up far more area than the back or trunk. Penfield & Boldrey 1937, *Brain* 60: 389-443; Penfield & Rasmussen, *The Cerebral Cortex of Man* (1950). Script says "the nineteen thirties", when the first maps were published.
- **Proprioception.** Muscle spindles, tendon organs and joint receptors signal limb position and movement without vision. Proske & Gandevia 2012, *Physiological Reviews* 92: 1651-1697.
- **Rubber hand illusion.** Botvinick & Cohen 1998, *Nature* 391: 756, "Rubber hands 'feel' touch that eyes see": real hand hidden, visible rubber hand, both stroked synchronously with paintbrushes (10 min in the original); participants reported feeling the touch on the rubber hand, and asynchronous stroking weakens it. Later studies find onset often within about 10-20 s (Ehrsson et al. 2004, *Science* 305: 875). "Within minutes, many people" is conservative.
- **Body-swap illusion.** Petkovic & Ehrsson 2008, *PLoS ONE* 3(12): e3832, "If I Were You: Perceptual Illusion of Body Swapping": head-mounted displays fed from cameras on a mannequin's head, or on the experimenter's head facing the participant; with synchronous touch / squeezing hands during a handshake, participants felt the other body as their own and "shook hands with themselves". The script's swap-back uses exactly this recipe (cameras on the other head + synchronous touch), which is why it is the solution.
- **Why a different body feels wrong.** The brain plans movements with an internal model of body size; different height and limb length mismatch it (body schema; Head & Holmes 1911; Haggard & Wolpert 2005 in *Higher-Order Motor Disorders*). "Different hormones" is stated plainly as a difference between bodies, no claim beyond that.
- **Grip force is set before contact from expectation.** Johansson & Westling 1984, *Exp. Brain Res.* 56: 550-564; Gordon, Forssberg, Johansson & Westling 1991, *Exp. Brain Res.* 83: 477-482 (grip force scaled to expected weight from visual size). Crushed cups are the story's exaggeration.
- **Bigger, more muscular bodies burn more energy at rest.** Resting metabolic rate scales mainly with fat-free mass. Cunningham 1991, *Am. J. Clin. Nutr.* 54: 963-969; Speakman & Selman 2003, *Proc. Nutr. Soc.* 62: 621-634. "Four breakfasts" is comic exaggeration.
- **Robert White's 1970 monkey head transplant.** White et al. 1971, *Surgery* 70: 135-139 (operation performed 1970): the transplanted head could see, hear, taste and track objects, but the animal was paralysed below the neck because the spinal cord was not (and cannot yet be) reconnected. Kept non-graphic in script and visuals (cartoon portrait card, spine icon).
- **Canavero's human head transplant.** Sergio Canavero announced the HEAVEN project (2013, *Surg. Neurol. Int.* 4: S335) and in 2017 claimed a rehearsal on a corpse in China; no human head transplant on a living person has ever been performed. Script: "a surgeon announced the first human head transplant. It never happened." (surgeon not named on screen).
- **Fiction flagged in-story:** "In this world, the rig didn't just fool them. It moved them." The seven-day lock, the swap itself and the swap back are fiction; the illusions they are built on are real.
