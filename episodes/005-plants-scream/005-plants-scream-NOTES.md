## Fact check
- Khait et al., *Cell* 2023 (Tel Aviv University, Hadany lab): drought-stressed tomato and tobacco plants made about 35 sounds/hour; cut plants also clicked; unstressed plants made under 1/hour. About 60 dB SPL at 10 cm. Mostly 40–80 kHz (humans hear up to 20 kHz). A machine-learning model told drought from cutting by sound.
- Likely cause is cavitation (air bubbles in the xylem). Insects using the sounds is a hypothesis, stated as unproven.
- Veits et al., *Ecology Letters* 2019: evening primrose made sweeter nectar within 3 minutes of hearing bee wingbeats.
- Appel & Cocroft, *Oecologia* 2014: Arabidopsis increased its chemical defences after playback of caterpillar chewing vibrations.
- Gagliano et al., *Oecologia* 2017: pea roots grew toward the sound of water in a sealed pipe.
- Turlings et al., *Science* 1990: caterpillar-damaged corn releases volatiles that attract parasitic wasps.
- Tree xylem acoustic emissions during drought have been recorded since the 1960s.
- Cut-grass smell = green leaf volatiles released on damage. Grass clicking is hedged ("if grass clicks like...").
