## Fact check
- **Visual reaction time is roughly 0.2–0.25 seconds; the ruler-drop test.** A dropped object falls about 30 cm in 0.25 s (d = ½gt²). Source: Kosinski, "A Literature Review on Reaction Time" (Clemson University, 2013); standard physics.
- **Anticipation beats reaction.** Knowing when an event will happen lets people respond with near-zero delay. Source: Schmidt & Lee, *Motor Control and Learning*, chapters on anticipation.
- **Memory consolidation takes hours and benefits from sleep.** Source: Squire et al., "Memory consolidation," *Cold Spring Harbor Perspectives in Biology* (2015); Rasch & Born, *Physiological Reviews* 93 (2013).
- **Working memory holds about four chunks.** Source: Cowan, "The magical number 4 in short-term memory," *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 24 (2001). (Miller 1956 proposed seven plus or minus two.)
- **Lorenz and the butterfly effect.** In 1961 Edward Lorenz re-entered a value rounded from 0.506127 to 0.506 and the weather simulation diverged; his 1972 AAAS talk was titled "Predictability: Does the Flap of a Butterfly's Wings in Brazil Set off a Tornado in Texas?" Source: Lorenz, *The Essence of Chaos* (1993); Gleick, *Chaos* (1987).
- **Lottery machines are chaotic systems.** Source: general chaos theory (Strogatz, *Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos*).
- **Simple pendulum is predictable; double pendulum is chaotic.** A simple pendulum's period depends (for small swings) only on its length. The double pendulum is the textbook chaotic system. Source: Strogatz, *Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos*; Shinbrot et al., "Chaos in a double pendulum," *American Journal of Physics* 60 (1992).
- **Pendulum clocks: the escapement releases the gear train one step per swing; stopping the pendulum stops the hands.** Source: National Watch and Clock Museum / British Horological Institute explanations of the anchor escapement.
- **Fiction, flagged in-script:** the rewind, "you can't stack rewinds," "only what's in working memory comes back," the headache cost, and "this old tower's minute hand jumps once a minute."
