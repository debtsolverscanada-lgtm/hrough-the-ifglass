## Fact check
- **Glasgow Coma Scale.** Teasdale & Jennett 1974, *The Lancet* 304: 81–84, "Assessment of coma and impaired consciousness: a practical scale". Eye opening (1–4), verbal response (1–5), motor response (1–6); total 3–15. A score of 8 or less is the common working definition of coma / severe brain injury (e.g. NICE head injury guideline NG232; Brain Trauma Foundation). Story keeps it non-graphic: only "squeeze my hand".
- **Hidden awareness ("cognitive motor dissociation") in about one in four unresponsive patients.** Bodien et al. 2024, *New England Journal of Medicine* 391: 598–608, "Cognitive Motor Dissociation in Disorders of Consciousness": 353 adults at six centres; CMD found in 60 of 241 (25%) who showed no behavioural response to commands, using task-based fMRI and/or EEG. Caveat noted by authors: convenience sample, so not a global prevalence. Script says "about one in four... still showed signs of hidden awareness in brain scans".
- **Tennis vs house.** Owen et al. 2006, *Science* 313: 1402, "Detecting awareness in the vegetative state": a 23-year-old woman, five months after a traffic accident, meeting the criteria for vegetative state; imagining playing tennis activated the supplementary motor area; imagining walking through her home activated parahippocampal gyrus, posterior parietal cortex and lateral premotor cortex, indistinguishable from healthy volunteers.
- **Yes/no code.** Monti et al. 2010, *NEJM* 362: 579–589, "Willful modulation of brain activity in disorders of consciousness": 54 patients (Cambridge and Liège); 5 could wilfully modulate brain activity; one patient used motor imagery (tennis) vs spatial imagery (navigating a familiar place) to answer yes/no questions, correct on 5 of 6 (no detectable answer on the 6th). Each answer needed a scan of several minutes of alternating imagery and rest, so "each answer takes minutes of imagining".
- **MRI scanners are loud** (knocking/hammering from the gradient coils; patients are given ear protection/headphones). NHS, "MRI scan: what happens".
- **Sound reaching the brain in unresponsive patients; talking is encouraged.** Di et al. 2007, *Neurology* 68: 895–899, "Cerebral response to patient's own name in the vegetative and minimally conscious states" (fMRI responses to the patient's own name spoken by a familiar voice); Blundon et al. 2020, *Scientific Reports* 10: 10336 (EEG responses to sound in unresponsive patients at the end of life). Families are commonly encouraged to talk to the person: NHS, "Disorders of consciousness" / Headway UK, "Coma and reduced awareness states". Wording kept careful: "sound can still reach the brain of patients who can't respond. That's one reason doctors encourage families to keep talking."
- **Familiar voice / own name stronger response.** Di et al. 2007 (above): own name by a familiar voice produced stronger activation than a neutral/unfamiliar condition in some patients. Script: "a familiar voice saying their own name can trigger a stronger response than a stranger's voice" (hedged with "can").
- **Familiar stories study.** Pape et al. 2015, *Neurorehabilitation and Neural Repair* 29(6): 537–547, Familiar Auditory Sensory Training (FAST): randomised placebo-controlled, 15 patients with traumatic brain injury in vegetative/minimally conscious states; recorded family stories 4×/day for 6 weeks; faster recovery of consciousness vs silence. Script says "a small study".
- **Jean-Dominique Bauby.** Editor of French *Elle*; brainstem stroke December 1995 left him with locked-in syndrome, able to move only his left eyelid. Dictated *Le Scaphandre et le Papillon* (The Diving Bell and the Butterfly, 1997) by blinking as an assistant recited a frequency-ordered alphabet (E S A R I N T U L O M D P C F B V H G J Q Z Y X K W). Alphabet board in the twist is the same technique.
- **Amnesia for the moments before a head injury** (retrograde / post-traumatic amnesia) is common. Headway UK, "Post-traumatic amnesia"; NHS.
- **Meteorites are older than Earth's oceans.** Most meteorites are about 4.56 billion years old (age of the Solar System; CAIs dated to ~4.567 Gyr, Connelly et al. 2012, *Science* 338: 651); Earth's oceans formed later (zircon evidence for liquid water ~4.4 Gyr, Wilde et al. 2001, *Nature* 409: 175). The Kestrel Meteorite itself is fictional.
- **Fiction flagged:** "in this world, Glint is wide awake inside a body that won't answer" (real comas are usually not like this; hidden awareness is found mainly in vegetative/minimally conscious states). Mr. Hale, the theft, the timings, the 200-mile transfer and the scanner slot are made up. Recovery speed is compressed for the story.
