## Fact check
- **Ophiocordyceps unilateralis ("zombie-ant fungus")** infects carpenter ants (Camponotus) in tropical forests, makes them leave the colony, climb vegetation and bite into a leaf vein ("death grip"); the fungal stalk grows from the ant's head and releases spores onto the forest floor below. Andersen et al. 2009, *American Naturalist* 174: 424-433; Hughes et al. 2011, *BMC Ecology* 11:13; Hughes, Araújo et al. (Penn State) on Thai and Brazilian species.
- **Toxoplasma gondii** makes infected rats lose their aversion to cat odour, and some appear attracted to it ("fatal attraction"). Berdoy, Webster & Macdonald 2000, *Proc. R. Soc. B* 267: 1591-1594; Vyas et al. 2007, *PNAS* 104: 6442-6447.
- **Rabies** spreads mainly through saliva, usually via a bite; it can cause agitation/aggression and hydrophobia, where attempts to swallow trigger painful throat spasms so patients panic at the sight of water. WHO Rabies fact sheet; CDC "Rabies: signs and symptoms".
- **Incubation / pre-symptomatic spread:** people with flu can infect others about one day before symptoms start. CDC, "How flu spreads".
- **R0:** basic reproduction number, the average number of people one case infects in a fully susceptible population; above one an outbreak grows, below one it shrinks. Measles R0 is classically quoted as 12-18 (Guerra et al. 2017, *Lancet Infect. Dis.* 17: e420-e428, reviews the range); seasonal flu median about 1.28 (Biggerstaff et al. 2014, *BMC Infect. Dis.* 14: 480).
- **SIR model:** susceptible, infected, removed (recovered or otherwise no longer infectious); infection rate depends on contacts between S and I. Kermack & McKendrick 1927, *Proc. R. Soc. A* 115: 700-721.
- **Quarantine length is based on the maximum incubation period** (e.g. 21 days for Ebola). CDC/WHO quarantine guidance.
- **Cornell zombie model:** Alemi, Bierbaum, Myers & Sethna (Cornell University), "You Can Run, You Can Hide: The Epidemiology and Statistical Mechanics of Zombies", *Physical Review E* 92, 052801 (2015), arXiv:1503.01104, presented at the APS March Meeting 2015. Simulated an outbreak across the continental US with real population data: dense cities fall quickly, remote areas take weeks to months to reach, and the safest region was the northern Rocky Mountains (e.g. northern Montana).
- **Walking speed:** a slow older walker at about 3 km/h covers 1 km in 20 minutes (simple arithmetic; typical older-adult gait speeds are around 0.8-1.2 m/s, so 3 km/h is deliberately slow for Nan's weak heart).
- **Fiction flagged in-story:** "In this story, the disease is made up. People call it the Drift." The Drift's three-day hiding period, R of three, attraction to noise, fear of water, the treatment and the island evacuation are all fiction built on the real mechanisms above.
