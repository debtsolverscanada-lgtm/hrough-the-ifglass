Glint has twenty minutes to get Nan onto the last boat out of a city full of the infected.

And Nan can't run. Her heart won't let her.

Between them and the harbour is Market Square. Three hundred infected people stand there, swaying under a flickering red emergency light.

Kai grabs Glint's arm. Kai says, "We sprint through. Now."

Glint says, "No. Whatever happens, nobody runs."

Let's go back. Day one.

At the city science museum, Dr. Osei is unpacking a new exhibit called Mind Control. In a glass case sit dozens of tiny ants, each one frozen, biting onto a leaf, with a thin stalk growing out of its head.

On the radio, a strange story. The hospital is full of people with a fever who won't sit still. They wander. They stare at bright lights. They walk straight towards noise.

Kai texts Glint one word. Zombies. With seven laughing faces.

By midnight, the sirens start.

Dr. Osei taps the glass case and says, "Nature has done this before."

<break time="1.0s" />

Here are the rules.

In this story, the disease is made up. People call it the Drift. But every trick it uses is borrowed from something real.

Trick one, from a fungus. In tropical forests, a fungus called Ophiocordyceps infects carpenter ants. It makes the ant leave its nest, climb a plant, and bite down on a leaf. Then the fungus grows out of the ant and rains spores on the ants below.

Trick two, from a parasite. Toxoplasma makes infected rats lose their fear of cats. Some even seem drawn to the smell.

Trick three, from rabies. Rabies spreads through saliva, usually a bite. And it can make swallowing so painful that patients panic at the sight of water.

So in this world, the infected are slow, fearless, drawn to noise and crowds. They spread it through saliva, a bite or a cough up close. And they won't go near water.

But they aren't monsters. They're patients. In this world, there's a treatment, if doctors can reach them.

You don't beat a disease by fighting it. You beat it by understanding how it spreads.

<break time="1.0s" />

Minus: Day two. The silent part.

In this world, the Drift hides for three days before any symptoms show. That's the incubation period. And during it, people are already passing it on. That part is real for plenty of diseases. People with flu can spread it a day before they feel ill.

So the most dangerous person in the city isn't the one swaying in the street. It's the one who feels fine.

Nan's neighbour, Mr. Hale, waves from his window that morning. Cheerful. Rosy cheeks. Healthy. By Day four, he's swaying in the street, staring at a car alarm.

Plus: Day three. The number that matters.

Dr. Osei draws one number on the museum whiteboard. R nought. It means how many people, on average, one sick person infects.

Measles is one of the highest ever measured, at twelve to eighteen. Seasonal flu is usually somewhere near one point three. In this story, the Drift starts at about three.

Dr. Osei says, "If it's above one, it grows. Below one, it dies out. Every closed door pushes it down."

So Nan's flat becomes a fortress. Doors shut. Windows taped. Nan bakes. A lot.

Minus: Day five. Kai's terrible idea.

Kai says, "Big supermarket. Everyone's going. We should go too."

That's exactly why not. Scientists who model epidemics, using what's called the S I R model, split everyone into three groups. Susceptible, infected, and removed. The more often the susceptible bump into the infected, the faster the infected group grows. A packed supermarket is where R jumps.

Kai goes anyway. He comes back with forty tins of beans and a man's cough in his face.

Plus: quarantine.

Glint doesn't panic. He uses the rule. If the Drift hides for three days, then Kai stays apart for longer than that, just to be sure. Real quarantines work the same way, based on how long a disease can hide.

So Kai lives in Nan's garden shed for five days. He plays the same three chords on his guitar. He's fine. Mr. Pickles is not.

Plus: Day nine. Dr. Osei calls on a crackly radio.

Back in two thousand and fifteen, a team from Cornell University ran a computer model of a zombie outbreak across the whole United States, using real population numbers. Dense cities fell fastest. The safest place they found was the remote northern Rocky Mountains. In their model, the outbreak took months to reach the quietest places, because there were so few people there to pass it on.

Dr. Osei says, "The lesson is real. Crowds are the danger. Space is safety."

Minus: Day ten. The announcement.

The city will evacuate by boat, to an island the infected can't reach, because they won't cross water. The last boat leaves Day twelve, at six in the evening.

The harbour is a long walk from Nan's flat.

And Nan can't run.

<break time="1.0s" />

Day twelve. Five forty.

They've walked for two hours. Nan leans on Glint's arm, step by step, pills in her pocket, a tin of flapjacks under her arm.

Then they reach Market Square.

Three hundred infected. Shuffling. Swaying. Filling every street to the harbour.

Twenty minutes.

Glint does the maths. Nan walks about three kilometres an hour. The harbour is one kilometre past the square. That's twenty minutes, exactly. No time to go around.

Somewhere, a dog barks. The whole crowd turns towards it at once, like one creature. That's the rule, live. Noise pulls them in.

Kai says, "Okay, new plan. We run."

Glint shakes his head. Running is loud. Noise pulls them in. And Nan's heart could stop before the infected even reach her.

Nan sits down on a bench. She says, "Go without me, love. Both of you."

Glint says, "Not happening."

Eighteen minutes.

Then he remembers the rules. The infected are drawn to noise. And they won't go near water.

Behind them, by the bins, is an abandoned ice cream van.

<break time="1.0s" />

Glint turns to Kai and says, "You know your worst idea ever? I need it now."

Kai grins. He climbs into the van, finds the keys under the sun visor, and switches on the music. The jingle blares across the square. Loud. Tinny. Unbearable.

Three hundred heads turn.

Kai drives slowly up the hill, away from the harbour. And the crowd follows him, like the Pied Piper.

Fifteen minutes.

A few stragglers stay behind. So Glint takes Nan the one way they won't follow. Along the edge of the fountain, then down the canal path, right beside the water.

Slow steps. No running. No noise. Nan's careful little shuffle is the quietest sound in the city.

Ten minutes. A straggler sways towards them, then stops at the water's edge, and turns away.

Five minutes.

The boat's horn sounds. The ramp starts to lift.

Glint waves both arms. A sailor sees them and stops the ramp.

Nan steps aboard at five fifty nine.

But Kai never comes back.

<break time="1.0s" />

Day thirty.

The boat comes home. Glint scans the dock, heart pounding.

And there's Kai, eating an ice cream, on top of the van.

He didn't just drive away. He drove all three hundred infected people straight up the hill, into the hospital car park, where the doctors were waiting with the treatment. The biggest house call in history.

With them treated in one place, the chains of infection broke. The city's R fell below one.

The news calls him the Pied Piper of Market Square.

Nan hugs him so hard his ice cream falls off the cone. Mr. Pickles, still annoyed about the shed, refuses to look at him.

Kai says, "Told you. Best idea ever."
