## Fact check
- **Adenosine builds up during waking and drives sleep pressure; caffeine blocks adenosine receptors.** Source: Porkka-Heiskanen et al., *Science* 276 (1997); Fredholm et al., *Pharmacological Reviews* 51 (1999).
- **Adults need seven or more hours (seven to nine recommended).** Source: CDC, "About Sleep"; American Academy of Sleep Medicine consensus, *Sleep* 38 (2015).
- **Sleep consolidates memories; hippocampal "replay" in sleeping rats.** Source: Wilson & McNaughton, *Science* 265 (1994); Rasch & Born, *Physiological Reviews* 93 (2013).
- **Glymphatic clearance: interstitial space increases about 60% during sleep in mice, speeding waste clearance.** Source: Xie et al., "Sleep drives metabolite clearance from the adult brain," *Science* 342 (2013); Iliff et al., *Science Translational Medicine* (2012).
- **Randy Gardner, 1964:** 17-year-old San Diego student, about 264 hours (11 days) awake for a science fair; reported moodiness, concentration and memory problems, and hallucinations; slept about 14 hours afterward and recovered. Guinness no longer accepts sleep-deprivation records. Source: Gulevich, Dement & Johnson, *Archives of General Psychiatry* 15 (1966); Guinness World Records statements.
- **Microsleeps: brief, involuntary lapses of a few seconds, often unnoticed; a major factor in drowsy driving.** Source: Poudel et al., *NeuroImage* (2014); NHTSA, "Drowsy Driving."
- **Fatal familial insomnia** is a very rare inherited prion disease that disrupts sleep. Mentioned only as context, explicitly separated from Glint's fictional power. Source: NIH Genetic and Rare Diseases Information Center (GARD); Montagna et al., *Lancet Neurology* 2 (2003).
- **Carbon monoxide:** colourless and odourless; binds haemoglobin about 200–250 times more strongly than oxygen; symptoms include headache, dizziness, confusion and drowsiness; sleeping people can be overcome without waking; especially dangerous for people with heart disease; working CO alarms recommended near fuel-burning appliances. Source: CDC, "Carbon Monoxide Poisoning: Frequently Asked Questions"; NHS, "Carbon monoxide poisoning."
- **CO mixes nearly evenly with air (density about 0.97 of air), so staying low doesn't help the way it does with smoke; leave and get fresh air.** Source: US EPA / CPSC CO guidance; NFPA.
- **Fiction, flagged in-script:** the machine, "the power switches the signal off completely," and "the memories were waiting to be filed" ("in this world"). The real takeaway is preserved: sleep after learning helps memory.
