# 028: What If You Woke Up and EVERYONE Was Gone?

Voiceover: `028-everyone-gone-VOICEOVER.txt` (about 1,390 spoken words, about 9 min). Same ElevenLabs voice and settings. One MP3.

**Logline:** Glint finishes a night shift and walks out into a world where every other person has vanished. Using the real
science of what fails, and in what order, when nobody is left to run a city, he works out that the most dangerous thing
left is the used-fuel pool at the nuclear plant upriver, whose backup diesel is about to run out. He can't refuel it
forever, so he makes gravity do the job. Six months later, when the new urban forest drops its leaves, a second window
lights up across the city and blinks a word only one person would send.

## Fact check
- **The grid must balance supply and demand every second; unattended it would fail within hours to a day or two.**
  Grid frequency control: US EIA, "Electricity explained: how electricity is delivered" and NERC balancing standards
  (BAL-001); ENTSO-E frequency containment. Unattended-world estimates: Alan Weisman, *The World Without Us* (2007),
  ch. on power; Popular Mechanics / National Geographic "Aftermath: Population Zero" (2008) experts: most fossil
  plants trip within hours, grid dark within about a day. Story says "a day or two" and the grid fails "on the second
  night".
- **Phone masts and traffic lights run on backup batteries for hours.** FCC 2007 Katrina Panel / FCC backup-power order
  (2020-2023) proposed 8 hours (and 24 h for some sites) of backup at cell sites; many sites have less.
- **Fridge 4 hours, full freezer 48 hours (24 h half full).** USDA FSIS, "Keeping Food Safe During an Emergency".
- **Canned food lasts for years.** USDA FSIS, "Shelf-Stable Food Safety": high-acid cans 12-18 months, low-acid cans
  2-5 years best quality, and safe indefinitely if the can is undamaged.
- **New York subway pumps about 13 million gallons a day; without pumps tunnels flood within days.** MTA (NYC Transit)
  figure of ~13 million gallons pumped on a dry day; Weisman, *The World Without Us* (2007), interviews with NYC Transit
  hydraulics staff: without pumps, parts of the system would flood within about 36 hours. Story keeps it to "within
  days, the tunnels start to fill".
- **1994 Northridge earthquake blackout: Los Angeles residents called about a strange "silver cloud", the Milky Way.**
  Widely reported by Griffith Observatory staff (e.g. Ed Krupp) and in light-pollution literature (International
  Dark-Sky Association; Bogard, *The End of Night*, 2013). Quake struck 4:31 a.m., 17 Jan 1994.
- **Reactors shut down automatically within seconds on loss of offsite power (control rods drop in).** US NRC, "Reactor
  scram" glossary; NRC backgrounder on station blackout (10 CFR 50.63).
- **Spent (used) fuel pools are about 12 m (40 ft) deep, keep fuel cool and shield radiation; used fuel stays hot for
  years.** US NRC, "Backgrounder on Spent Fuel Pools" and "Spent Fuel Storage in Pools and Dry Casks". Without cooling,
  pool water heats, boils, and over days to weeks could boil down; uncovered fuel can overheat and release
  radioactivity (NRC NUREG-1738, 2001; NRC spent fuel pool study NUREG-2161, 2014). Timings in story ("in days, it
  boils. Over weeks, the water boils away") are the plausible range for a pool with recent fuel.
- **About seven days of diesel on site for emergency generators (US).** NRC Regulatory Guide 1.137 / ANSI/ANS-59.51:
  seven-day onsite fuel oil storage for emergency diesel generators. Story says "in the United States".
- **After Fukushima (2011), plants added ways to add water to the fuel pool from outside with portable equipment and
  hose connections.** NRC Order EA-12-049 (Mitigation Strategies, "FLEX") and EA-12-051 (spent fuel pool
  instrumentation); NEI 12-06. At Fukushima Daiichi itself, fire engines and a concrete pump truck sprayed water into
  the Unit 4 pool (TEPCO, 2011).
- **About one extra atmosphere of pressure per 10 m of water height; water towers work by height alone.** Hydrostatic
  pressure p = ρgh: 1000 kg/m³ × 9.81 × 10.3 m ≈ 101 kPa ≈ 1 atm. US EPA / AWWA water tower explainers.
- **Cherenkov light: the blue glow in pools around recently used fuel.** US NRC photo gallery and Idaho/Oak Ridge
  national lab explainers; P. A. Cherenkov, Nobel Prize in Physics 1958.
- **Chernobyl exclusion zone wildlife rebounded after people left in 1986: wolves, elk, wild boar.** Deryabina et al.
  2015, *Current Biology* 25: R824-R826, "Long-term census data reveal abundant wildlife populations at Chernobyl"
  (elk, roe deer, red deer, wild boar similar to nature reserves; wolves about seven times higher). Weisman (2007) on
  cities going wild: plants in pavement cracks, freeze-thaw.
- **Morse code letters.** International Morse code (ITU-R M.1677-1).
- **Fiction flagged in-story:** the vanishing itself ("In this world, nobody knows why it happened"); the reservoir
  height ("In this story, its surface is forty metres higher than the pool"); one person laying the hose line in a
  few hours and the gravity feed being enough to keep the pool topped up is story licence; the plant, the city and
  the Morse message are invented.
