## Fact check
- **Prairie dog alarm calls encode predator type, size and colour.** Slobodchikoff et al. 1991, *Animal Behaviour* 42: 713–719 (semantic information distinguishing predators); Kiriazis & Slobodchikoff 2006, *Behavioural Processes* (different calls for hawk, coyote, dog, human); Slobodchikoff, Paseka & Verdolin 2009, *Animal Cognition* 12: 435–439, "Prairie dog alarm calls encode labels about predator colors" (calls differed when the same person walked through in different coloured shirts); Slobodchikoff, *Chasing Doctor Dolittle* (2012). Gunnison's prairie dogs, northern Arizona.
- **Adult cats rarely meow at each other; meowing is mainly directed at humans.** Nicastro 2004, *J. Comparative Psychology* 118: 44–50; Bradshaw, *Cat Sense* (2013). Mr. Pickles' translations are comic fiction.
- **Slow blink is a friendly cat signal.** Humphrey et al. 2020, *Scientific Reports* 10: 16503, "The role of cat eye narrowing movements in cat–human communication".
- **Fledgling birds on the ground are usually fine and watched by parents.** RSPB, "What to do if you find a baby bird".
- **Crows recognise human faces, remember them for years, and learn from other crows.** Marzluff et al. 2010, *Animal Behaviour* 79: 699–707, "Lasting recognition of threatening people by wild American crows"; Cornell, Marzluff & Pecoraro 2012, *Proc. R. Soc. B* 279: 499–508 (crows never captured learned to scold the mask socially). Scolding of the "dangerous" mask continued for many years (the study ran over a decade; widely reported in 2024). Story uses this with British crows; the face-recognition finding is from American crows.
- **Mobbing:** crows gather and call at a threat, recruiting others. Marzluff & Angell, *Gifts of the Crow* (2012); general corvid behaviour literature.
- **Honeybee waggle dance** encodes direction (angle of the waggle run relative to vertical = angle of food relative to the sun) and distance (duration of the waggle run). Karl von Frisch, Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine 1973; Seeley, *Honeybee Democracy* (2010).
- **Sperm whale codas and clan dialects.** Codas are stereotyped click patterns; vocal clans have distinct coda repertoires. Rendell & Whitehead 2003, *Proc. R. Soc. B*; Hersh et al. 2022, *PNAS* (identity codas as symbolic clan markers); Sharma et al. 2024, *Nature Communications* 15: 3617 (Project CETI, Dominica). "Here, family, here" is Glint's in-story interpretation, not a claim of translation.
- **Fiction flagged in-story:** the power itself ("In this world, Glint doesn't hear animals speaking English. He decodes what animals actually communicate"); all translated words; the crows switching to "peanut face".
