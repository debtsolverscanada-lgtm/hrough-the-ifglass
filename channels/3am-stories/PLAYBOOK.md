# Playbook: animation, script, hooks, editing, thumbnails (all three channels)
Distilled from the owner's 12 study videos (Nov 2026). Rules only; numbers are defaults, deviate on purpose.

## 1. Packaging first (before any script)
- Write in this order: one-line idea → title → thumbnail idea → bullet outline → intro → body → outro.
- Outline gut-check: if no point/scare is new or surprising, research more instead of writing.
- Plan title + thumbnail together; note thumbnail text in brackets after the title. Never repeat the title in the thumbnail.

## 2. Script
- Shorts: ~150-170 words for 45-55 s (about 3 words/s), one idea per clause, "you" or a named everyman.
- Line 1 = click confirmation: deliver what the title/thumbnail promised, then add one step worse.
- Long-form intro (first 30-45 s): context → what people assume → the twist on it → proof (date, place, record) → plan ("three things happened that night").
- Order beats: second-best scare first, best second, then build to the ending. A weak first beat loses viewers.
- Every beat: what happened → show it up close → why it matters to the bigger mystery; end with a re-hook ("that wasn't the worst part").
- Every line must make you want the next one. One short sentence per line, 5-12 words.
- Shorts turn: "here's the weird part" / "Wait..." then "You see," + an everyday analogy built as a picture; last line lands the twist and ideally loops into the first line.
- True stories: source every claim; when sources disagree use the safe wording; sources in the description.
- Long-form chapters are named like cliffhangers (viewers scan chapter titles before leaving).

## 3. Animation (engine defaults now follow these)
- Holds are dead still: no idle bob or sway; life comes from the camera, blinks, faces and props.
- The camera never stops: steady creep ~5%/s, no handheld wobble (shake only on impact moments).
- Transitions: 0.2 s dissolve on the first word of the new sentence, both shots still moving. Whip (p.whip: true) only for shocks; hard cuts almost never.
- A new picture about every 1-2 s in Shorts (about one per clause); 25-30 shots per 50 s.
- Facts are objects: every number/time/date lands as a thing on its exact word (clock, counter, calendar, poster), plus the pop-up labels for name/age/place.
- Every place and object named is drawn (see RULES.md). Sets feel lived in; never an empty floor.
- Faces change through a blink, landing ~2 frames before the stressed word.
- Frame check: no face in the caption band (66-76% of height), never crop the eyes, dark scenes must still read on a phone.
- One art style per channel, but a new cast, grade and setting every video (RULES.md).

## 4. Sound
- Voice tight: ~0.2 s after a sentence, never above ~0.3 s, except one deliberate silence before the scare.
- Effects only on visible actions, 12-20 a minute: impacts ~10 LU under the voice, whooshes ~15, ticks ~22. No sharp bings.
- Music/drone ~18 LU under the voice; master -14 LUFS. Licensed or synthesised only.

## 5. Editing long-form
- First minute cuts about twice as often as the rest.
- Plant an open loop early ("later I'll show you what was on the recording") and pay it off late.
- Re-hook at every section change; a recurring "case file" / map card marks each new chapter.
- Mid-video asks (subscribe, comment) only after a payoff, never before.

## 6. Thumbnails
- One clear idea, one wrong thing (figure in a doorway, door ajar). No clutter.
- Text: 0-3 words (cut words until it breaks, put the last one back). Try no text on the strongest images.
- Nothing important bottom-right (timecode). Balance the frame; an intentional empty side is a horror tool.
- What should be seen first goes on the left. Design for phone size and rounded corners.
- Must be true: open the video on the thumbnail's image within 5 s.

## 7. Staying monetisable
- No reused templates: new characters, grade, scenery and story every video; research and sources visible.
- AI is the co-pilot, not the whole video: real research, real choices, a human check of every video before upload.
- YouTube asks for an "altered or synthetic content" label on realistic AI media; animated stories are generally fine, but tick it when a video shows realistic AI people/events.

## Pre-publish checklist
1. Title + thumbnail planned together, thumbnail text ≠ title. 2. Line 1 confirms the title. 3. First beat is a strong scare.
4. Every clause has its own picture; every named place/thing drawn; every number lands as an object.
5. Re-hooks at each section; twist on the last word. 6. New cast + grade not used in the last 10 videos.
7. Voice pauses ≤0.3 s; effects under the voice; -14 LUFS. 8. Faces clear of captions; nothing too dark on a phone.
9. Facts sourced (true stories) and sources in the description. 10. Watched in full before upload; one Short per 3 hours.
