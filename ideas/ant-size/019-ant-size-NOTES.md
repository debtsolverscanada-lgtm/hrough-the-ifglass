## Fact check
- **Square-cube law (Galileo, *Two New Sciences*, 1638).** Strength scales with muscle cross-sectional area (length²), weight with volume (length³), so smaller bodies are stronger relative to their weight. At 1/90 scale: area ÷ 8,100, volume ÷ 729,000. Source: Galileo (1638); Schmidt-Nielsen, *Scaling: Why Is Animal Size So Important?* (1984).
- **Sugar cube ≈ 4 g vs. ant-sized Glint ≈ 0.1 g (70 kg ÷ 729,000)**, so about 40x his weight. Simple arithmetic.
- **Many ants carry 10–50 times their body weight.** Source: Hölldobler & Wilson, *The Ants* (1990); Nguyen et al., *Journal of Biomechanics* 47 (2014) on ant neck-joint strength.
- **Surface tension traps small animals; insects rely on water-repellent hairs.** Source: Bush & Hu, "Walking on water: biolocomotion at the interface," *Annual Review of Fluid Mechanics* 38 (2006); Vogel, *Life in Moving Fluids*.
- **Small animals have low terminal velocity and survive falls; Haldane's mouse-down-a-mine-shaft line.** Source: J. B. S. Haldane, "On Being the Right Size" (1926); Vogel, *Comparative Biomechanics*.
- **Smaller vocal folds vibrate faster and produce higher pitch.** Source: Titze, *Principles of Voice Production*.
- **Smaller, faster-metabolism animals tend to perceive time faster (higher flicker-fusion rates; flies far above humans).** Source: Healy et al., "Metabolic rate and body size are linked with perception of temporal information," *Animal Behaviour* 86 (2013). Glint's brain speeding up is flagged "in this world."
- **Age-related hearing loss (presbycusis) affects the highest frequencies first.** Source: NIDCD (NIH), "Age-Related Hearing Loss."
- **Phone calls carry a limited frequency band** (classic narrowband telephony is about 300–3,400 Hz), so a very high, faint voice transmits poorly. Source: ITU-T G.711 / standard telephony references.
- **Small bodies lose heat quickly (high surface-area-to-volume); shrews must eat almost constantly.** Source: Schmidt-Nielsen, *Animal Physiology*; Taylor, "Body size and metabolism" reviews.
- **Cats hear up to about 64 kHz, far above humans (about 20 kHz); mice use high-pitched and ultrasonic calls.** Source: Heffner & Heffner, "Hearing range of the domestic cat," *Hearing Research* 19 (1985).
- **Smoking oil can ignite; pan fires are a leading cause of kitchen fires; turn off the heat, cover with a lid, don't move it, never use water.** Source: NFPA, "Cooking safety"; UK Fire Service / London Fire Brigade cooking-fire guidance.
- **Mother cats carry kittens by the scruff.** Source: general feline behaviour (e.g., Bradshaw, *Cat Sense*). The idea that Mr. Pickles heard Glint as a kitten is phrased as "maybe," not as fact.
