## Fact check
- **Röntgen discovered X-rays in November 1895**; his early radiograph of Anna Bertha Röntgen's hand (Dec 1895) shows bones and her ring. Early X-ray (Crookes-type) tubes from 1896 survive in museum collections. Source: Science Museum Group collection; Nobel Prize in Physics 1901 biography.
- **Eyes can't see X-rays; X-ray imaging needs a source and a detector.** Human vision covers roughly 380 to 750 nm; X-ray images are made by passing X-rays through the body onto a detector. Source: NIBIB (NIH), "X-rays" fact sheet; OpenStax *College Physics* 24 and 32.
- **Bone (calcium) and metal absorb more X-rays than soft tissue,** so bone and metal show clearly and soft tissue faintly. Source: NIBIB "X-rays"; RadiologyInfo.org (RSNA/ACR).
- **Backscatter airport scanners** detected X-rays scattered back from the body; dose per scan about 0.05 to 0.1 microsievert, comparable to roughly one to two minutes of flying at cruising altitude. Most airports now use non-ionising millimetre-wave scanners instead (hence "used to use"). Source: US FDA, "Products for Security Screening of People"; NCRP Commentary 16; UK Health Security Agency.
- **X-rays are ionising radiation and can damage DNA; risk adds up with dose.** Source: US EPA "Radiation Basics"; WHO "Ionizing radiation and health effects".
- **Chest X-ray ≈ 0.1 mSv ≈ about 10 days of natural background radiation.** Source: RadiologyInfo.org, "Radiation Dose" table.
- **Radiation badges (dosimeters) worn by hospital staff.** Source: UK HSE Ionising Radiations Regulations 2017 guidance; NRC.
- **Lead glass blocks X-rays but transmits light;** used in X-ray room viewing windows and radiation-protection eyewear. Source: IAEA Radiation Protection of Patients, "Protective eyewear"; manufacturer specs (lead-equivalent glass).
- **Time, distance, shielding** are the three basic principles of radiation protection; for a point source, dose rate falls with the square of distance (double the distance, quarter the dose). Source: US CDC "Radiation Emergencies: Time, Distance, Shielding"; inverse-square law, OpenStax *College Physics*.
- **Concrete shields X-rays** (used for X-ray and CT room walls). Source: NCRP Report 147, *Structural Shielding Design for Medical X-Ray Imaging Facilities*.
- **Titanium plates and screws show clearly on X-rays.** Source: RadiologyInfo.org; any orthopaedic radiology text.
- **Museums X-ray and CT-scan fossils and mummies non-destructively.** Source: Natural History Museum London (CT imaging of fossils); British Museum mummy CT scanning projects.
- **Thermal cameras can't see through concrete.** Infrared is blocked by solid walls. Source: FLIR technical notes; basic optics.
- **Eye lens is one of the most radiosensitive tissues; radiation can cause cataracts.** ICRP 2011 Statement on Tissue Reactions (lowered the occupational eye-lens dose limit to 20 mSv/year); ICRP Publication 118.
- **Fiction flagged in-story:** "In this world, the rule is: Glint's eyes do both jobs." The specific dose numbers for Glint's gaze, the green flash, and the exact rubble scenario are story.
