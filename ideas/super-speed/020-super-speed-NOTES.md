## Fact check
- **Speed of sound** about 343 m/s (about 1,235 km/h) in air at 20 °C; **speed of light** about 299,792 km/s, roughly 870,000 times faster ("almost a million times"). Source: NIST / any intro physics textbook (e.g. OpenStax *College Physics*, ch. 17 and 24).
- **A voice stretched 100x becomes inaudible:** adult speaking voices have fundamental frequencies of roughly 85 to 255 Hz; divided by 100 they fall to about 1 to 3 Hz, well below the roughly 20 Hz lower limit of human hearing. Source: OpenStax *College Physics* 17.6 (hearing range); Titze, *Principles of Voice Production*. In-story stretch: we assume Glint's ears and brain also run at 100x, so he hears the world's sounds slowed.
- **Air drag grows with the square of speed** (twice as fast, four times the force): drag equation, F = ½ρv²C_dA. Source: NASA Glenn Research Center, "The Drag Equation".
- **Sonic boom** is a shock wave from an object moving faster than sound, and booms have broken windows (documented for Concorde and military aircraft). Source: NASA Armstrong fact sheet "Sonic Booms"; FAA.
- **Friction turns motion into heat**, and frictional heating rises with sliding speed. Source: OpenStax *College Physics* 6.5 and 7 (work-energy, friction).
- **Kettle** takes about 3 to 4 minutes for a full kettle (3 kW). 3 min x 100 = 300 min = 5 h. Arithmetic.
- **Falling glass:** from about 0.9 m, t = sqrt(2h/g) ≈ 0.43 s, so "less than half a second"; x100 ≈ 43 s. Free-fall kinematics.
- **Calories:** UK guideline intake about 2,500 kcal/day for men, 2,000 for women. Source: NHS, "Understanding calories". The 100x per real hour of speed follows from the premise (his metabolism runs at 100x). Six cinnamon buns ≈ 2,000 kcal (a large bun is about 300 to 400 kcal; USDA FoodData Central).
- **Honeybee wingbeat** about 230 beats per second. Source: Altshuler et al. 2005, *PNAS*, "Short-amplitude high-frequency wing strokes determine the aerodynamics of honeybee flight".
- **Sudden acceleration injures people** (basis of whiplash and crash injuries). Source: NHTSA crash biomechanics; any physiology text on g-forces. Moving someone several metres in a few milliseconds implies accelerations of thousands of g.
- **Momentum** (mass x velocity): a 12-tonne lorry at even 30 km/h carries about 100,000 kg·m/s. Arithmetic.
- **Runaway truck ramps / arrester beds** use deep loose gravel so wheels sink and the truck's energy is absorbed. Source: US Federal Highway Administration, "Truck Escape Ramps" (FHWA Technical Advisory T 5040.38); UK Highways England design standard CD 109 / arrester beds.
- **Fiction flagged in-story:** the 100x speed itself, its trigger ("when his heart races"), and that his body survives the forces. The grandad line is a story hook, not a science claim.
