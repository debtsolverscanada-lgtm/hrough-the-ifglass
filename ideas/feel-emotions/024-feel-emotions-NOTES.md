## Fact check
| Claim in script | Source |
|---|---|
| Emotional contagion: people automatically mimic faces, voices and posture, and the feedback nudges their own feelings | Hatfield, Cacioppo & Rapson, *Emotional Contagion* (Cambridge University Press, 1994); Hatfield et al. (1993), *Current Directions in Psychological Science* |
| Mirror-touch synesthesia affects about 1.6% of people | Banissy, Cohen Kadosh, Maus, Walsh & Ward (2009), "Prevalence, characteristics and a neurocognitive model of mirror-touch synaesthesia", *Experimental Brain Research* 198 |
| Mirror neurons found in macaque monkeys in the 1990s in Italy (Parma) | di Pellegrino et al. (1992), *Experimental Brain Research*; Gallese, Fadiga, Fogassi & Rizzolatti (1996), *Brain* |
| Scientists debate how much mirror neurons explain human empathy (script hedges this) | Hickok, *The Myth of Mirror Neurons* (2014); Heyes & Catmur (2022), *Perspectives on Psychological Science* |
| Newborns cry more in response to another newborn's cry | Sagi & Hoffman (1976), *Developmental Psychology* 12; Simner (1971), *Developmental Psychology* 5 |
| Adrenaline is released within seconds of stress; cortisol rises over minutes and can stay elevated for an hour or more | Harvard Health Publishing, "Understanding the stress response" (2020); Dickerson & Kemeny (2004), *Psychological Bulletin* (cortisol peaks ~20 to 40 min after stressor, returns to baseline over ~40 to 60+ min) |
| Stress hormones raise heart rate and force; acute stress can be risky for people with heart disease | American Heart Association, "Stress and Heart Health"; Harvard Health "Understanding the stress response" |
| Above about 5 people per square metre, crowds become dangerous and act like a fluid with pressure waves ("crowd turbulence") | Prof. G. Keith Still, *Introduction to Crowd Science* (CRC Press, 2014); Helbing, Johansson & Al-Abideen (2007), *Physical Review E* 75 |
| Crowd safety advice: arms up in front of chest to protect breathing space, move diagonally/sideways with the flow, not against it | G. Keith Still crowd safety guidance; UK Health and Safety Executive, *Managing crowds safely* (HSG154) context |
| Slow breathing with longer exhales can lower heart rate and calm the stress response | Zaccaro et al. (2018), "How breath-control can change your life", *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience* |
