Ninety seconds to midnight.

Forty thousand people are packed along the river, waiting for the fireworks. And Glint can feel every single one of them.

Excitement. Cold feet. Impatience. And underneath it all, rising like a tide, something worse.

Panic.

It's coming from the bridge stairs. Where Nan is. And her heart is not built for this.

Let's go back.

Three weeks earlier, the science museum unveils a brand-new exhibit. The Mirror Room. You put on a soft cap covered in sensors, and a giant screen shows your brain activity as swirling colours. Happy is gold. Stressed is red.

Glint is testing it on the night shift. Nan's cat, Mr. Pickles, has come along, because Nan says he gets lonely. Glint does not believe this.

Mr. Pickles jumps onto the control desk. Knocks over a full mug of tea. The screen fizzes. The cap on Glint's head gives a sharp little zap.

And suddenly, Glint feels... annoyed.

Not his annoyance. He loves this job.

He turns around. Mr. Pickles is glaring at him, soaking wet.

He's feeling the cat.

<break time="1.0s" />

So here are the rules.

Some of this is real, everyday science. Humans catch each other's feelings all the time. It's called emotional contagion. Without noticing, we copy each other's faces, voices and posture, and copying a face can nudge us toward feeling what it shows.

Some people go further. About one point six percent of people have something called mirror-touch synesthesia. When they see someone else being touched, they feel it on their own body.

And in the nineteen nineties, scientists in Italy found brain cells in monkeys that fired both when a monkey grabbed something, and when it watched someone else grab it. They called them mirror neurons. Scientists debate how much they explain human empathy.

In this world, Glint has all of it, turned up to full. He feels what people around him feel, as if it were his own. The closer they are, the stronger it gets.

There's no off switch.

<break time="1.0s" />

Plus: Nan can't hide anything anymore.

She says she's fine. She always says she's fine. But Glint walks into the kitchen and feels a heavy, grey worry sitting in his own chest. Then he sees the counter. Three trays of scones. Nan bakes when she's worried. She has a heart check-up on Friday, and she hasn't told anyone. Glint sits down, takes her hand, and says he'll come with her. Nan doesn't argue. That's how he knows how scared she was.

Minus: he feels pain that isn't his.

Kai tries to jump a bin on his skateboard. He does not jump the bin. Kai scrapes his knee, and Glint, standing three metres away, yelps and grabs his own leg. Kai laughs so hard he forgets it hurts. Glint does not forget.

Plus: he's the best lost-and-found in the city.

On a busy Saturday at the museum, he feels a spike of terror from two halls away. Sharp and small. He follows it, past the whale skeleton, and finds a little girl who's lost her dad. She's back in his arms in four minutes. Feelings spread from the very start of life. Newborn babies cry more when they hear another newborn crying.

Minus: crowds are torture.

Rush hour. Fifty strangers on one bus. Late, stressed, tired, cross. When you're stressed, your body releases adrenaline within seconds, and cortisol over the next few minutes. Cortisol can stay high for an hour or more. Glint catches everyone's stress, and his body reacts to all of it. By the time he gets home, his hands are shaking, and he hasn't done anything.

Plus: almost everyone is an open book.

Except Kai. Kai feels like fireworks. Loud, bright, all at once. Glint can never tell what's underneath. Kai says that's because there's nothing underneath. Kai says he's simple. Glint isn't sure.

Minus: he can't find himself.

Dr. Osei notices first. Glint laughs when the person next to him laughs. Frowns when they frown. He's stopped knowing which feelings are his. Dr. Osei says, "Feeling everything is not the same as understanding it."

So on New Year's Eve, when Nan wants to see the fireworks from the bridge, Glint can't go into that crowd. He sends Kai to look after her.

He stays at the edge.

Ninety seconds to midnight.

<break time="1.0s" />

The bridge stairs are a bottleneck. Too many people coming down, too many going up. And at the bottom, a side gate is locked.

Glint feels the pressure before he sees it. It hits him like a wave. Squeezed chests. Fear. Then more fear, as people catch it from each other.

Crowd scientists warn that once a crowd gets denser than about five people per square metre, it starts to behave like a liquid. Waves of pressure ripple through it. People can't choose where they go anymore.

And Nan is in the middle of it.

He feels her clearly now, because she's family. Her fear is fluttering, tight, fast. Stress hormones make the heart beat harder and faster. For a weak heart, that's dangerous.

Sixty seconds to midnight. When the fireworks start, everyone on the bridge will push forward to see.

Glint calls Kai. Kai's voice is loud, cheerful. "We're fine, just a bit squashed!"

Glint isn't fooled. He can feel what's under Kai's voice for once. A thin, cold thread of something Kai never lets show.

Then the line goes dead. Too many phones in one place.

Forty-five seconds.

Every instinct Glint has says stay out. Inside that crowd, he won't just see the fear. He'll become it.

He walks into the crowd.

<break time="1.0s" />

Every step, the fear gets louder. Thousands of people's panic, pouring into one body. His knees nearly give.

So Glint uses the one feeling he can control. His own.

He breathes in slowly. And out, even slower. Long, slow breathing helps calm the body's alarm system. His heart slows. The noise in his head drops, just enough to think.

And now the power isn't a curse. It's a map.

The fear isn't everywhere. It's strongest in one spot, like heat on a weather chart. The bottom of the stairs. At the locked gate. That's where the pressure starts.

He follows the heat. He finds a steward by the gate, frozen, terrified, holding the key. She's not allowed to open it without orders.

Glint feels her fear. So he knows exactly what she needs. Not a shout. A calm face. A steady voice. Emotions are contagious. Calm is too.

"Open it," he says gently. "I'll stay right here."

She turns the key.

The crowd spills out through the gate like water from a bath. Glint feels the pressure drain away. Fear fading into surprise. Then relief.

He spots Kai on the stairs, Nan tucked beside him, and shouts across the heads. Arms up in front of your chest. Protect your breathing space. Move sideways with the crowd, not against it.

Midnight.

The fireworks explode over the river. And Glint feels forty thousand people say "wow" at the exact same time.

<break time="1.0s" />

He finds Nan on a bench, pink-cheeked, scolding Kai for squashing her hat. Her heart is calm. She's okay.

Then Kai sits down. And for the first time, the fireworks inside him go quiet.

Underneath, there's fear. Huge, freezing fear. Kai was terrified the whole time.

Kai, who is never afraid, was the most scared person on that bridge.

And he walked Nan through it anyway, joking the whole way.

That's what brave feels like from the inside.

By morning, the power fades, like an echo. Glint panics, just a little. How will he know what anyone's feeling now?

Nan pours the tea. Nan says, "Love, I've known what you're feeling for nineteen years. No cap required. You just look."
